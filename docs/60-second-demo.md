# 60-second demo (for a recruiter)

The listener is not an engineer and is not a salesperson. In one minute they need four things: what this
is, what problem it solves, why it is relevant to a GTM Engineer role, and what I personally built.
Nothing else fits, so nothing else is here.

**No statistics. No acronyms without a plain-English gloss in the same sentence.**

---

## The script

**152 words · about 61 seconds at 150 words per minute · 6 sentences.**

> GTMOS is a system that tells a sales team which companies to call, and why today rather than next
> month.
>
> Most sales teams answer that with a spreadsheet and four tools that don't talk to each other, so good
> leads go cold while nobody notices.
>
> I built the part in the middle: the code that reads what companies are doing, works out which ones
> matter, picks who owns them, and pushes that into the CRM — where reps keep track of their deals —
> automatically, and in a way you can check.
>
> That middle part is the job: a GTM Engineer is the engineer who builds and runs the systems a revenue
> team sells with.
>
> I designed and built all of it — the database, the decision logic, the automation, the web app and
> the connections to outside tools.
>
> And where it doesn't work yet, it says so out loud, which was the harder half.

### Delivery notes

- Sentence two is the one that lands. Pause after "nobody notices" and let it sit.
- Do not accelerate through sentence three. It is the list of things I built; it should sound like a
  list, not a clause.
- The last sentence is not modesty. It is the strongest claim in the script and should be said flatly,
  as a fact about the work.

## The one screenshot

**The flagship account page, scrolled to *Why this score*:**
`http://localhost:3010/accounts/c5c23567-74a1-5483-bc14-224b4eb057e6`

One frame, and it should contain: the company name and grade (**Kestrel Analytics · A 98**), the five
category bars, and at least three of the plain-English reason rows underneath, for example —

- *Industry 15 / 15 — AI/ML Platforms is a core ICP industry.*
- *Company size 12 / 12 — 850 employees is inside the sweet spot (250–5,000).*
- *AI/ML hiring surge 8.8 / 10 — 14 open AI/ML roles, observed 6 days ago.*

Why this one and not the dashboard: a recruiter can read those sentences without being told what they
mean. A wall of statistics needs narration; this does not. It shows a machine giving a reason, which is
the entire pitch in a single image.

Crop out the left navigation if the image is going in a document. Leave the **DEMO** banner in.

## If they ask one follow-up

**"Is it live — can I try it?"**
It runs on a laptop with one command, and it has never sent an email or a message to anyone, by design.

**"Is that real company data?"**
No — it is two thousand invented companies on reserved test domains, generated by the project itself, so
nothing in it describes a real customer or a real result.

**"How much of it did you build?"**
All of it: the database, the backend, the website and the tests. The four outside tools it plugs into
are off-the-shelf, and only one of those four has actually been connected to the real service — the
project says which, on its own screen, instead of claiming four.

---

## What not to say

- Do not say it "uses AI to find leads". It uses hand-written rules for every decision; the only place
  text is generated, a human has to approve the result before anything can happen with it.
- Do not quote a success metric. There isn't one: no message was ever sent, so there is no outcome to
  report, and inventing one would undo the point of the last sentence in the script.
- Do not name the four tools unless asked. To this listener they are four logos, and the sentence costs
  ten seconds it cannot spare.
